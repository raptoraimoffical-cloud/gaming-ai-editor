# Short-form engagement research and editing changes

Reviewed 13 primary/platform sources on 12 October 2026. The two-second interval is the requested design target; the reviewed evidence does not establish an exact universal optimum or guarantee retention. The source inventory, evidence type and limitations are in [shortform-engagement-research.json](../electron/data/shortform-engagement-research.json).

## Findings used in the editor

| Evidence | Editing implication |
|---|---|
| YouTube names choosing to view, viewing duration/percentage and satisfaction signals in Shorts discovery. | Establish the actual subject quickly and deliver a satisfying conclusion. No private algorithm weights or promised views. [Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225), [recommendations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387) |
| The platform's interview with Jenny Hoyos emphasizes immediate hooks and concise miniature stories. | Aim for a relevant foreground picture in the opening second and preserve setup/action/payoff. This is creator experience, not a controlled one-second law. [YouTube interview](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) |
| YouTube distinguishes starts/replays, engaged viewing and subsequent viewing behavior. Its retention guide says spikes can also indicate confusion. | Report delivered timeline gaps honestly; keep information legible rather than forcing speech repetition or making evidence difficult to read. [Engagement metrics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9313698), [retention interpretation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415), [comparable Shorts analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217) |
| TikTok recommends hook/body/close, movement, transitions and purposeful sound; its 2026 playbook contains a worked example with brief clips. | Use frequent meaningful developments and phase-timed sound, adapted to this story. These are ad-production examples, not a gaming Shorts retention guarantee. [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_CreativeCodes_May2023.pdf), [2026 playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/AUNZ_Creative_Starter_Pack_TakeItToTikTok.pdf) |
| TikTok/System1's sponsored research examines repeated ad exposure, creative variety and entertainment. | Vary staging and relevant humor; avoid repeating an identical intro or gag across fresh versions. Its advertising findings cannot predict a particular gaming upload. [Creative effectiveness](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-effectiveness) |
| Wals and colleagues analyze 2,520 viewing experiences across 42 public-service announcements. Shorter, simpler scenes show stronger attentional synchrony; visual complexity has a delayed negative association. | Use one dominant focus with coordinated support and frequent purposeful developments. This supports clarity alongside activity, not filling every corner with competing effects. The outcome is attention synchrony in ads, not Shorts watch-through probability. [Original paper](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1033011/files/1033011.pdf) |
| Valuch and colleagues find that repeated content across cuts supports attention relocation in their sports-video task. | Keep the lead and spatial continuity while supporting imagery changes. [Original experiment](https://csc.univie.ac.at/paper/ValAnsBucPatSch14.pdf) |
| Gaze-based video retargeting preserves salient regions and cinematic constraints. A separate webpage study examines highlighting under cognitive load. | Keep faces, characters and evidence visible; coordinate a dominant highlight rather than making every layer demand attention simultaneously. This is a design inference across different viewing tasks. [Video retargeting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03125), [HCEye](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14232) |

## Changes that execute

Quick Draft and the full creative planner author long scenes in roughly eight-second passages while retaining the complete current story and instructions. Individual long passages avoid a large combined schema response. Their local word indices are validated, then mapped back to the original scene. The previous six-action limit applies per manageable response, rather than discarding everything after the first six actions of a long scene. Narration, caption groups and scene boundaries are not split.

Each passage gets narration-aligned engagement windows and a clear hook/development/payoff brief. Source-image recovery can restore a resolved, actually spoken subject missing from the composition, in a supporting lane at its real cue. It avoids duplicates, future subjects, active fights and manual deletions. Scenery, atmospheric particles, changed captions and sound alone do not count as meaningful foreground developments.

The final timeline audit measures actual source-picture arrivals and substantial authored actions, carrying its clock across sentence boundaries. Readable evidence and complete actions/footage receive explicit holds. It flags quiet stretches, missing opening imagery and unintended early named reveals. The Pipeline evidence panel recomputes these checks after edits; its numbers describe the composition, not predicted viewer retention.

Cadence accents now respond to quiet gaps and actual spoken cues instead of restarting an arbitrary timer at each sentence. They do not keep generating pops through post-narration silence. Chart panels, labels and context framing begin at the actual narrated data cue and end after the explanation, with the original cast restored outside that interval. Reapplying chart staging preserves attachments and avoids accumulating layout changes.

Live checking also exposed the default inference transport's five-minute response deadline. Completion requests now use an explicit dispatcher with header/body deadlines disabled, while keeping the existing Stop/window-close signal and discovery/resource checks. This follows the [Undici client timeout contract](https://github.com/nodejs/undici/blob/main/docs/docs/api/Client.md). Recipe quotes are constrained to actual passage text; irrelevant chart indices are cleared when no chart is requested, and unsupported chart metadata cannot discard valid actions.

## Verification

`tests/shortform-engagement.test.mjs` covers complete long-scene coverage, late actions, raw-word alignment, name-span boundaries, actual visual gaps, source recovery, manual controls, chart timing and untouched captions. `scripts/verify-shortform-engagement.mjs` uses a real configured model and original cached Pokémon art to plan a synthetic continuous scene and render its later actions. Its narration is muted and no editor project is saved. Existing Google acquisition, original recording/caption appearance and explicit Save behavior remain in use.

The live Qwen3:8b check accepted all three passage responses and delivered 13 native action events throughout a 24-second continuous scene, including actions after 16 seconds. Eight production-rendered frames were inspected, with captions unchanged. Its pacing audit still reported two quiet intervals, demonstrating that the checker does not claim every trained response meets the target. The production desktop check also verified tab navigation during AI Changes, preview updates and explicit Stop; two independent remakes of one real voiceover preserved its narration/captions without saving either project.

There is no audience experiment for this editor update. The existing retention import can inform review from a user's historical curve; new-video performance still needs real upload analytics.
