# Fresh creative edits and sourced visuals

Creative AI Changes such as “more images,” “animate the narration,” and style/humor requests now enter trained planning instead of shortcuts that disabled AI. Explicit AI opt-out and direct property edits keep their existing paths. New requests for more animation or sound supersede obsolete quiet/silent preferences, and Undo restores those settings. Captions and source narration remain protected.

The creative model considers two or three ideas in context, then selects compact scene recipes. The animator expands them into measured prop attachments, handoffs, launches, drops, reactions, fast movement and size comparisons. This reduces generated coordinate data and rejects unknown actors or invented facts. Standalone Google photos and GIFs can keep their background; held props still need suitable cutouts. Surrounding sentences and earlier ideas support setup/payoff and discourage repeated gags. If the preferred model fails, a configured reasoning model can take over with its actual identity and limitations reported.

Unnamed Pokémon hooks now use black silhouettes of upcoming resolved Pokémon with question marks until their spoken reveal. Named openings choose question, challenge, comparison, reversal or spotlight staging from the narration. Quiet intervals receive purposeful image motion around every two seconds; readable evidence, manual compositions and complete actions retain their holds. Pops, swooshes, game clicks and other existing sound assets follow delivered visuals, including transfers and focus changes. Automatic source-card layouts give the description and character separate space. Collision checks also sample animation/keyframe peaks.

Move recognition handles split names and conservative local spelling recovery against the 937 bundled move records plus the live mechanic catalogue. Narrated checks, counters and threats can choreograph an illustrative attack with the specified move's real type/damage class; negations and learning descriptions are guarded. Google searches now also retrieve verified ability descriptions, with a second screenshot-oriented query and cached inspections. Production finishing omits invented description popups when no readable source image is available. The old eight-search default migrates to 32; configured lower limits remain adjustable, and keys are unchanged.

Latest verification: the real Qwen3:8b model proposed three alternatives and a valid compact crown-handoff recipe. Actual artwork was rendered as mystery silhouettes and as held, travelling and received props. A separate replay renders the recorded real-model proposal through the updated compiler. Source-description and Google GIF protocols were tested with controlled provider fixtures, without repeating live Google searches. The diagnostic transcript is synthetic and uses existing real artwork; it is not an edited user export. Reports are in `workspace/creative-story-check`.

Generating a new video from a previously used voiceover also starts a new creative pass. The director runs again for every generation; persistent AI composition caches are no longer read or written, including caches left by older versions. Downloaded artwork and transcription caches remain available. Reusing narration never restores a saved edit automatically.

Whole-video remakes now rebuild automatic scenes and plan new staging. They no longer restore old quick-patch pixels or accumulate historical AI Changes commands into the current brief. The model receives its previous creative choices as context to avoid copying them. Undo retains the previous project. Selected time patches still preserve everything outside their range. Downloaded source files can be reused; editing plans are generated again.

The configured trained editing model can author narration-driven actor and prop actions, using exact transcript quotes, real actor identities, Google prop queries, attachment points, relative keyframes and detachment times. This supports combinations beyond a fixed list of expressions. For example, a dethroning scene can establish a crown, approach the outgoing character, then knock the sourced crown away. Unique quoted phrases are aligned directly to the transcript. Released props get visible flight when a model would otherwise make them disappear, and actors return smoothly to their original camera path. Missing props or invalid plans are reported rather than represented by pretend graphics.

Google Images is the first choice for Pokémon artwork, held items, move information images, creative props and online reaction searches when enabled and configured. Existing verified Google originals take priority over cached sprite fallbacks. Move-card images need a readable matching name and description, checked by an installed vision model. Moveset mentions can retrieve a sourced build image containing multiple moves; printed move names are checked against the Pokémon's available learnset. This is a sourced example, not proof of the statistically best set or current game-specific tournament legality. Attribution is retained. Sourced fire, lightning, water and ice artwork is animated when available.

The existing Google Images connection and credential configuration remain unchanged, as requested. Automated checks of Google priority, downloads, card verification, moveset filtering and caching used controlled provider fixtures; live Google queries were not repeated in this verification run.

Overlay layout uses visible artwork bounds, an 18-pixel gutter, caption reservations and sampled motion. Intentional actor/prop contact and attacks are preserved. Crowded layouts with no free space at a readable size are reported. Transparent image padding no longer pushes attached items away; scarves use neck anchors, hats use head anchors, and manual anchor adjustments override automatic ones. Exact inspected artwork can use content-hash-guarded anatomical landmarks. Other artwork uses validated vision landmarks when available, with a silhouette fallback when uncertain. Ground shadows follow actor motion and use actual fitted artwork coordinates. This improves placement but cannot guarantee perfect anatomy on every pose.
